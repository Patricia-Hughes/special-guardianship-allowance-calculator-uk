Most problems are resolved by asking clear questions in writing and getting a proper answer. The
complaints route is for when that has not worked.

## Step 1: your council's own procedure

Councils have a formal complaints procedure, usually with two or three stages. Use it. The
Ombudsman will normally expect you to have been through it before it looks at anything.

Keep it factual and specific:

- what you asked for, and when
- what the council did or did not do
- what effect it has had on you and your child
- what you would like to happen

## Step 2: the Ombudsman

In England, that is the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman. Wales, Scotland and Northern
Ireland each have their own.

Normally you should complain within **12 months** of becoming aware of the problem, though the
Ombudsman has discretion where there is a good reason for delay.

## What it has ordered

These are real outcomes in special guardianship allowance cases, not hypotheticals:

**Recalculation and refunds for around 170 carers**, plus remedy payments to the complainants,
where a council deducted Child Benefit as a blanket rule including from carers on means-tested
benefits.

<Cite id="lgsco-devon-2024" />

**Backdating**, where a council's fixed-percentage policy was found to be at fault.

<Cite id="lgsco-north-tyneside-17002928" />

**A finding of fault** where a council tapered a whole allowance to 75% and then 50%, misapplying
the rule that protects only the reward element.

<Cite id="lgsco-southend-2020" />

## Before you start

Gather the paperwork: the written policy, any breakdown of your calculation, and your
correspondence. A complaint with documents attached is a great deal harder to answer with a general
reassurance.
