## Two different calendars

<Callout type="important">
There are three dates in April, not two. Councils normally raise allowance rates on **1 April**.
The government states those same rates for a **tax year running 6 April to 5 April**. Benefit rates
change in the **first full week of April**, around the 6th. Between the 1st and the 6th, the new
allowance rates coexist with last year's benefit rates, and a council following the tax year may
still be paying last year's allowance.
</Callout>

Those gaps are a real source of confusion, including inside councils. My calculator treats
allowances and benefits as two independent lookups with their own effective dates, so a calculation
dated 3 April uses the new allowance rates and the old benefit rates. That is correct, and it is not
what you get if you assume one financial year covers both.

I follow the council convention of 1 April, because that is what decides what a carer is actually
paid. If you are working out what you were owed over past years, the site tells you in pounds how
much of the total depends on that choice. It is a few pounds per April, not a few hundred.

## Why I do not publish figures I have not verified

I hold rate data as versioned files with an effective date, and the build fails if a figure looks
wrong, if rates fall as age rises, if a value sits outside a sane range, or if a year-on-year
change is implausible.

Where a figure has not been confirmed against its original source, I do not publish it. That is
why one year of Welsh rates is missing: working a historic rate backwards from an uplift percentage
would produce a number that looks precise and could be a few pounds out, and a few pounds out
across two years of backdating is exactly the kind of error that gets a carer's complaint
dismissed.

The Welsh rates are an exception worth explaining, because they look wrong. In Wales a child under
five attracts a higher rate than a child aged five to fifteen, which is the reverse of every other
table I hold.

I have now seen the Welsh Government's own circular, which prints them that way, so this is settled
rather than inferred. I publish them as published and explain them on screen, rather than quietly
correcting a government figure to look more sensible.

## What rises are expected

Committed uplifts to the fostering national minimum allowance are the higher of 2.5% or CPI for
2027/28 and 2028/29. I do not publish projections as though they were rates.
