<Callout type="critical">
Special guardianship orders **do not exist in Scots law**. If you are caring for a relative's child
in Scotland, the English rules on this site (regulation 6, regulation 7 and the statutory
guidance) do not apply to you.
</Callout>

My calculator routes Scotland to a different set of figures for exactly this reason, and it will
not attach English case law to a Scottish result.

## Three things that follow from it

**It is a floor, not a ceiling.** Your council can pay more, and some do. If you are being offered
less than the recommended rate, ask why in writing.

**Foster and kinship carers get the same recommended rate.** If your council pays its foster carers
more than it pays you for a child of the same age, that is a difference worth asking it to explain.

**No family should be worse off.** Where you already have an arrangement paying more than the
recommended allowance, the higher amount applies. Moving onto the recommended rate should not
reduce what you receive.

## One boundary worth knowing

The recommended allowance covers young people aged 16 to 18 who remain **in care**. It does not
cover **continuing care** arrangements, which are a separate scheme with their own rules. If your
young person is 16 or over, it is worth being clear which of the two applies to them before you
quote a figure at anybody.

## Where to get help in Scotland

The Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman covers England only. Scotland has the Scottish
Public Services Ombudsman. Citizens Advice Scotland and local kinship care groups can help with the
Scottish system specifically.
