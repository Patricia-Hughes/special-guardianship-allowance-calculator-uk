A special guardianship order ends when the young person turns 18. In England, financial support normally ends too, but regulation 9 includes an exception concerning continuing full-time education or training. Ask the council for a written decision before the birthday, rather than assuming support must stop or will automatically continue.

I have not reached this stage myself. What follows is what the regulation says and what to ask for,
not something I have tested.

The [allowance calculator](/) and [published age-band tables](/rates) can help check support before 18. They do not calculate an adult entitlement or decide whether the education or training exception applies.

## The exception

Where the young person remains in full-time education or training, support can continue. This is
not automatic and it is not always offered, it often has to be asked for.

<Callout type="important">
Ask **before** the eighteenth birthday, not after. Several months before is not too early.
</Callout>

## What to ask

1. What happens to my support on my child's eighteenth birthday?
2. If they stay in education or training, will it continue, and for how long?
3. What do you need from me, and by when?

Ask for the answer in writing. It is the document you will need if payments stop unexpectedly.

## Other things that change at 18

The young person may be able to claim benefits in their own right, and your own Child Benefit and
Universal Credit will be affected. This is a good moment to get a full benefits check from an
adviser, the changes interact, and a check often finds something.

## What you can check, and what you have to ask for

Regulation 9 is the rule. It is published, and it is what brings both the order and the support to
an end.

Whether support carries on while the young person stays in education is your council's decision. It
is not automatic, and how it decides is published nowhere. Ask months ahead, and ask for the answer
in writing.
