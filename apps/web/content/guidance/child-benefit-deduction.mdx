If your allowance seems low, this is one of the first things worth checking. It is also one of the
most commonly contested.

## Start with what is allowed

The regulations **require** a council to take into account any other grant, benefit, allowance or
resource available to you. They do not name Child Benefit, but Child Benefit is plainly one of
them, so it is something your council has to consider.

<Cite id="sg-regs-2005-reg13" />

The deduction itself comes from the case law rather than from that wording. In *Barrett v Kirklees*
the court said councils should pay at a rate in line with the fostering allowance, "with the
deduction of Child Benefit if appropriate".

<Cite id="kirklees-2010" />

So the deduction is permitted. If you ask your council whether it is allowed to do this, the answer
is yes, and the conversation stops there. That is why this page starts here rather than with the
complaint.

It is worth being precise about this, because a council that is quoted the wrong rule will correct
you, and you will have spent your first letter losing a point you did not need to make.

## What some councils do

A number of councils treat Child Benefit as income when they work out a special guardianship
allowance, and take it off pound for pound. So a carer who would otherwise get the full amount
receives that much less each week.

## Where the real question lies

In 2024 the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman looked at a council that deducted Child
Benefit from every carer on Universal Credit as a matter of blanket policy, without looking at
individual circumstances. The Ombudsman found that at fault. The council had to recalculate the
allowances of around 170 carers, pay refunds, and make remedy payments to the carers who
complained. It changed its policy, and a later complaint about the new practice was not upheld.

<Cite id="lgsco-devon-2024" />

The point was never that Child Benefit can be ignored. It was that applying one rule to everybody,
without thinking about the individual case, is not the same thing as assessing someone's
circumstances, which is what the regulations require. In another decision the Ombudsman put the test
as whether the council had justification for the deduction.

So the question worth asking is not **may you do this**. It is **how did you decide it in my case,
and what were your reasons**.

<Callout type="critical">
If you receive Universal Credit, Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance,
income-related Employment and Support Allowance or Pension Credit, **and** your council deducts
Child Benefit from your allowance, this is worth raising in writing.
</Callout>

## What to ask your council

Keep it short and ask for answers in writing:

1. Do you take Child Benefit into account when calculating my allowance, and if so how much?
2. Does your policy treat carers on means-tested benefits differently?
3. How was that decided in my case?

Asking for the written policy at the same time saves a round of correspondence.

## If you get nowhere

Use your council's complaints procedure first, the Ombudsman will normally expect you to have
done that. If you are still not satisfied, you can ask the Ombudsman to look at it.
