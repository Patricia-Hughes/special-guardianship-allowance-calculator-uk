Financial support is subject to review. In practice, this is one of the easiest things to let slip
and one of the easiest to put right.

## What actually triggers a review

This is worth getting right, because it changes the question you ask.

Your council has to review your financial support **when it receives your annual statement of
circumstances**. The trigger is that statement, not simply a year going by.

<Cite id="sg-regs-2005-reg18" />

Supplying that statement is something you agree to when support is set up.

<Cite id="sg-regs-2005-reg10" />

So if your council has never asked you for an annual statement, you are not late for a review. Your
council has not set up the thing that produces one. That is a stronger point to make than "it has
been more than a year", and it is the question to put in writing: **have you asked me for an annual
statement, and if not, why has my support not been reviewed?**

## What a review should involve

- The council asks you for up-to-date information about your circumstances.
- It works out whether the support should change.
- It tells you what it proposes, **before** it changes anything.
- It gives you a reasonable chance to comment.
- It tells you the outcome, with reasons.

## Why it matters more than it sounds

Councils normally raise their rates on **1 April** every year. (The government states the same
rates for a tax year beginning 6 April, which is explained on [the rates
page](/guidance/rates).)

<RateTable nation="england" />

An allowance that has not been reviewed in several years has quietly lost value against those
figures. Nothing dramatic has to have gone wrong for you to be materially underpaid. It is enough
for nobody to have looked.

## Asking for one

You do not need a reason beyond "it has been more than a year". Ask in writing, and ask what
information the council needs from you so it can get on with it.
