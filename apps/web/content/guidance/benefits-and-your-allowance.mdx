Two rules that catch people out, and one benefit that is widely missed.

## Your allowance is not taxed, and does not reduce your benefits

Special guardianship allowance is treated as a residence-order allowance. It has been **exempt from
income tax** since 6 April 2010, and it is **disregarded** as your income when your own means-tested
benefits are worked out.

If your Universal Credit dropped when your allowance started, that is worth querying.

This is not the same as the deduction I ran into. I was receiving Universal Credit when my order was
made, and what Child Benefit came off was **my council's calculation**, not my benefit. The two get
confused easily, and they are answered by different people.

<Callout type="important">
**Qualifying Care Relief does not apply to special guardians.** It is a relief for foster carers
and shared-lives carers. If you also still foster other children, you can use it for that income,
but not for your special guardianship allowance.
</Callout>

## Guardian's Allowance, check whether you can claim it

It is a separate claim, and it is not paid automatically alongside Child Benefit.

Guardian's Allowance is a weekly payment for someone bringing up a child whose parents have died,
or where one parent has died and the other cannot be traced, is serving a long prison sentence, or
whose whereabouts are unknown.

It is worth <BenefitAmount name="guardians-allowance" /> for each child, on top of anything else
you receive.

- It is **tax free**.
- It is **ignored** for means-tested benefits.
- It is **exempt from the benefit cap**.

<Cite id="cb-ga-uprating-order-2026" />

There are conditions, including about the parents' UK residence. It is worth a conversation with an
adviser if there is any chance it applies.

## The two-child limit has been abolished

From April 2026 the two-child limit in Universal Credit was removed.

This one is recent enough that I would check it against your own award notice rather than take my
word for it. Benefit rates on this site change in the first full week of April, not on 1 April like
your allowance, so for a few days each year the two do not line up.

<Cite id="uc-two-child-limit-act-2026" />

If you are caring for three or more children and your Universal Credit has not changed, check your
award.

## Child Benefit

Child Benefit for the child you care for is yours to claim. Whether your **council** may take it
into account when setting your allowance is [a separate and contested
question](/guidance/child-benefit-deduction). But the answer is never to stop claiming it.
