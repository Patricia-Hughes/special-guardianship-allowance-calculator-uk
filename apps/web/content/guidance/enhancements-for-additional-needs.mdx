Additional-needs support is financial help that takes account of a child's particular needs, rather than age alone. In England, regulation 6 allows support to reflect disability and the consequences of abuse or neglect. There is no single national enhancement amount on this site: the council needs to consider the child's circumstances.

Start with the [calculator's baseline estimate](/) and the [published age-band rates](/rates), then list the additional costs that baseline does not meet. The [guide to council assessments](/guidance/what-your-council-must-consider) explains why an individual decision matters.

## Why my calculator adds nothing for this

Because I would have to invent the number, and an invented figure is worse to you than no figure.

<Callout type="important">
There is **no national figure** for an enhancement. It varies entirely by council.
</Callout>

So I add nothing, and I tell you to ask instead. Applying an invented percentage would produce a
figure that looks authoritative and is not, and if you took it to your council, the first thing
that would happen is that it would be dismissed, along with everything else you said.

## What helps a request succeed

Be specific and put costs against things. A social worker deciding this needs something concrete to
work with:

- **Travel**: appointments, therapy, contact arrangements. Mileage and how often.
- **Therapy or support** you are paying for privately, and why.
- **Equipment** and adaptations.
- **Respite**: what you need and what it costs.
- **Extra wear and tear**: replacement bedding, clothing, damage.
- **Lost earnings** where you have had to reduce your hours.

Where the need arises from what happened to the child before they came to you, say so plainly. That
is squarely within what the regulation covers, and it is often left out because carers assume it
only means physical disability.

## If you are turned down

Ask for the reasons in writing. Then ask what evidence would change the answer. A refusal that
cannot explain itself is worth taking to the [complaints
procedure](/guidance/complaining-to-the-ombudsman).

## What you can check, and what you have to ask for

Regulation 6(2)(b) is cited above and it is short. A council may take a child's needs into account
when it decides financial support.

The amount is the part nobody can give you. No national figure exists, which is why my calculator
adds nothing here, and why a request has to carry your own costs with it.
