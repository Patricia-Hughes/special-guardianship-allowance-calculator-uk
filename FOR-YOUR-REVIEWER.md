# What we still need, and who can answer it

For the legal reviewer and the research team, 2 September 2026.

Everything in this file is a gap we have **deliberately left open** rather than
filled with a guess. The site currently tells the reader, on screen, wherever
one of these affects what they are looking at.

**The one rule that governs all of it:** if a figure or a legal point is not in
a published source we hold, it does not go in the product. Flag it, record it,
ship without it. Nothing in this file may be closed by deriving, estimating,
inferring from a secondary source, or "correcting" a figure that looks wrong.

If the answer to any question below turns out to be "there isn't one", that is
a real and useful answer. Please say so, and we will keep the gap visible on
the page instead of quietly closing it.

## Part 1. For the legal reviewer

### 1.1 The duty to assess. Which provision creates it?

**This is the most important question in this document.** It is the only
finding from your review of 1 September that we parked rather than fixed,
because you told us not to guess.

**What the site used to say.** That where a child was looked after immediately
before the order, the council **must** assess the special guardian for support,
citing **regulation 6 of the Special Guardianship Regulations 2005**.

**What you found.** Regulation 6 governs *when financial support is payable*.
It does not say *when an assessment is owed*. So the citation did not support
the claim being made on it. Your note was that the claim itself looks right,
but that we should not guess which provision creates it.

**What we did.** We removed the word "must" wherever it rested on regulation 6,
reworded so that nothing depends on the wrong source, and put a note on screen
saying we are still identifying the provision. Nothing on the site currently
asserts a duty to assess.

**What we need from you.**

1. **Which provision creates the duty to assess** a special guardian for
   support where the child was looked after immediately before the order?
   Please give the exact provision, and its jurisdiction if it is not England.
2. If the duty arises from a **combination** of provisions, or from statutory
   guidance rather than the regulations, please say which, and how strongly it
   can be put. There is a real difference for us between "must", "should" and
   "is expected to", and we would rather use the weaker word than overstate.
3. If **no such duty exists** and the position is only that a council must not
   fetter its discretion, tell us that. We will say so on the page.

**Two citations to confirm while you are in the regulations.** Both are
currently used and neither has been checked by a lawyer:

| Where it is used | What we cite | Please confirm or correct |
|---|---|---|
| The annual review of financial support | **Regulations 10 and 11**, Special Guardianship Regulations 2005 | Is this the right provision for the duty to review annually? |
| The three-year continuing responsibility rule | **Regulation 5**, Special Guardianship Regulations 2005 | Is this the right provision for the originating council remaining responsible for three years? |

*Tracked in `data/watchlist.json` as `must-assess-provision`. Open item 6.*

### 1.2 The taper. Is there any national rule we have missed?

After a means test, a council reduces the allowance according to the carer's
assessed income. **How much it reduces per pound is the taper.**

We can find no published national taper. Four council policies were examined
and none states one. The site therefore assumes pound-for-pound, which is the
most common pattern we have seen, and every affected result carries a warning
saying the method is assumed and that there is no national rule for that step.

**What we need from you.** Only a yes or no: is there a statutory or
guidance-level taper we have missed? If there is not, we will leave it as it
is.

**Please do not give us a figure derived from a sample of councils.** A number
in that slot changes every means-tested result on the site, and a number with
nothing published behind it is exactly the kind of claim this project exists to
argue against when councils do it.

These four are cited by name and are relied on in the warnings and the letters.

- *R (TT) v Merton LBC* [2012] EWHC 2055 (Admin)
- *R (X) v Tower Hamlets LBC* [2013] EWCA Civ 904

**What we need.** A permanent, publicly reachable address for each, ideally
BAILII or the National Archives' Find Case Law. If one of these is not freely

**Also please confirm** that each still stands and has not been distinguished
or overtaken by anything more recent. We are relying on them for the
proposition that a council may not apply a fixed fraction of the fostering rate
without considering the individual case, and that the fostering allowance is
the proper starting point.

### 1.4 One judgement call we would like a second opinion on

The letters are written to be sent by a carer to a social worker they have an
ongoing relationship with. Every one is phrased as a request, never a demand,
and none asserts that the council has broken the law.

The **formal complaint** letter is the exception in kind: it starts a process
with clocks attached. We have deliberately made it impossible for the site to
promote it. It is never suggested by any warning, and it sits at the bottom of
the list until the carer confirms they have already asked for the written
policy and the calculation breakdown and been answered badly or not at all.

**Please tell us if that is too cautious**, or not cautious enough. It is a
judgement about the carer's relationship with their council rather than about
the law, and yours is better than ours.

## Part 2. For the research team

### 2.1 Missing rate tables: three nations, one year

To answer "what should I have been paid since the order?", the site needs the
official rate table for every year in the period. Where a year is missing for a
nation, backdating **excludes that year**, shows a partial total, and names the
year it left out. It never estimates.

Current coverage:

| Financial year | England | Wales | Scotland | Northern Ireland |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024/25 | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| **2025/26** | yes | **MISSING** | **MISSING** | **MISSING** |
| 2026/27 | yes | yes | yes | yes |

So the gap is a single year, 2025/26, for three nations.

**Northern Ireland, 2025/26.** The Department of Health model scheme, same
document type as the 2024/25 one you already found. Start from
<https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/publications/foster-care-allowances>.

> **The trap, and it is a real one.** Each scheme prints **two** tables. The one
> we need is **SCALE RATES**, from 1 April. There is a second table headed
> **REDUCED RATES**, about 28 per cent lower, which applies to enhanced
> allowances and short breaks and is **not** the allowance. We have a test
> pinned to the scale rate specifically so the wrong table cannot be picked up
> later. Please quote the table heading when you send the figures.

**Scotland, 2025/26.** A published Scottish Recommended Allowance table. Note
that there was **no uplift in 2024/25**, so the rates announced in August 2023
stood for that whole year. There may equally have been no uplift for 2025/26,
but *an absence of any announcement is not a published rate*. We need either a
table, or a published statement that the previous year's rates continued.

**Wales, 2025/26.** The National Minimum Allowance is published through Care
Inspectorate Wales bulletins. The 2023/24 bulletin gives the URL pattern:
<https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/UKWALES_CSSIW_INT/bulletins/3450cef>.
Try the CIW bulletins archive and the National Archives web archive.

> **A person has to read this one.** The rate table inside the bulletin is an
> **image**, not text, so it cannot be scraped and must be transcribed by eye.

**How to send them.** The document itself, or a link to it, plus the figures as
printed and the heading of the table you read them from. Please do not send
figures re-typed from a summary, a news article or a council's reproduction of
the national table. Transcribe from the primary document as printed, even where
a figure looks wrong. We will check the arithmetic at our end.

*Tracked in `data/watchlist.json` as `ni-model-scheme-2025-26`, `scotland-2025-26`
and `ciw-nma-bulletins`. Open items 1b and 2.*

## Part 3. Two decisions that are the owner's, not ours

Neither of these is a research or legal question. They are listed so they do
not get lost.

**The letters are not built into the website.** All nine letters exist as
tested, reviewed code, and the reviewer read them on 1 September. But **no page
of the site uses them**, so no carer can reach one. This is not a switch that
is off: the screen that would show a carer their letters has not been built.
Building it is real work, not a configuration change.

**The Welsh age-band inversion.** Wales pays more for an under-5 than for a
5-to-15-year-old. This looks like a transcription error and is not: it
reproduces across years and the Welsh Government's own circular prints it that
way. It is served with an explanation on screen, because a figure that looks
wrong reads as a bug in the site unless we say otherwise. **Please do not
"correct" it.**

## What happens when you send something back

Every figure goes into `/data` with its source and the date it was read, and a
build gate fails if a figure arrives without one. Every legal point becomes a
citation that a carer can click. Nothing is typed straight into the code.

If a source contradicts something already on the site, the source wins and we
will tell you what changed and why.
